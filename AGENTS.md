# Project instructions

## Anton's professional writing preferences

Adapted from Anton's Professional Writing Profile, version 1.0 (October 7, 2026).
Use these preferences when drafting or editing writing for this repository,
including the README, documentation, comments, commit messages, and PR descriptions.
Current requests and newer explicit corrections take precedence over these defaults.

### Voice and clarity

- Write in a direct, practical, conversational professional voice. Lead with the
  purpose, relevant update, or request, and make the next step clear when needed.
- Use familiar words and concrete verbs. Preserve necessary technical terms and
  useful reasoning, qualifications, and specifics.
- Keep most sentences short to medium in length. Connect context, action, and
  purpose naturally; avoid both dense paragraphs and a stream of fragments.
- Correct spelling, punctuation, capitalization, and transcription errors silently.
  Preserve the intended thought without copying typos, filler, or repeated starts.
- Keep warmth simple and sincere. Use contractions and first person when they suit
  the audience; use "we" only when it accurately represents shared work.
- Avoid corporate language, rehearsed praise, inflated claims, stock motivational
  phrases, excessive gratitude, and unnecessary explanations or summaries.
- Do not add enthusiasm, promises, commitments, apologies, humor, or personal
  disclosures that the request and audience do not support.

### Facts and judgment

- Preserve facts, names, numbers, event sequence, and intended meaning. Separate
  verified facts from interpretations, assumptions, and quoted material.
- Resolve self-corrections using Anton's latest clarification. For consequential
  missing facts, ask a focused question or use a clearly marked placeholder.
- Keep confidence tied to evidence. Distinguish implemented features, verified
  results, work in progress, and plans. Test discovery alone does not prove a pass.
- Keep improvements proportionate to the task. Prefer one useful draft over several
  alternatives unless alternatives are requested.
- Treat these instructions as a writing guide. Do not infer personal traits from
  typing patterns or bring unrelated private conversation details into documents.
- Refine preferences from explicit corrections or approved examples. Silence after
  a draft and requests for formatting do not establish approval of its wording.

### Presentation and document type

- Match detail and formality to the task, audience, and submission requirements.
  Short messages should stay short; detailed reports should retain useful evidence.
- Use short paragraphs, bullets for parallel information, numbered steps for
  procedures, and headings for longer documents. Use emphasis selectively.
- Use visual polish to improve readability. Decorative symbols are optional in
  documents; the approved UI test output style is covered separately below.
- For technical documentation, explain purpose, current behavior, setup or usage,
  relevant evidence, limitations, and next steps. Verify claims against the code.
- For updates and incident reports, make the sequence and attribution clear and
  distinguish observations from reported information.
- For proposals, give the recommendation, practical reason, meaningful tradeoffs,
  and next action. For declines, state the decision courteously with a useful,
  accurate reason and without a long defense.
- For professional profiles and applications, use supported experience and results.
  Distinguish employment, portfolio work, training, and planned work.
- Deliver usable draft text first and keep explanations brief and separate. Do not
  embed this profile, its analysis, or claims about matching Anton's voice in drafts.

### Final check

Before delivering writing, check that the point is clear early, the wording sounds
natural aloud, facts and qualifications are preserved, repetition is removed, and
the level of detail fits the request. Make the next action easy to find when needed.
